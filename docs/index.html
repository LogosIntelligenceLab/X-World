01 / The problem & our approach

From watching the world
to acting in it.

Video diffusion models capture object motion, contact, and task progression across diverse scenes. Transferring these representations to robot control requires handling different camera configurations, heterogeneous action spaces, and continuous execution.

We present X-World, a framework that connects a pretrained video backbone to a shared action expert through a fixed-length token sequence. A residual cross-view branch supports one, two, or three views while preserving pretrained per-view self-attention. A layer-aligned Q-Former compresses the visual representation, and dataset-specific projections retain each robot’s native state and action interface.

Training separates video adaptation from action-only pretraining, followed by joint target-domain post-training. At control time, a single backbone pass extracts visual features, while asynchronous action chunking supports execution. We evaluate the transfer on LIBERO, seven LIBERO-Plus distribution shifts, and six real-world bimanual tasks.

02 / Variable views, a shared interface

Keep the video prior.
Connect it to control.

Camera processing and action decoding meet through a fixed-length token sequence, allowing the two interfaces to adapt separately.

01

Adapt across views

Residual cross-view attention, view embeddings, randomized view order, and masked replication support heterogeneous camera inputs.

02

Compress the visual context

A layer-aligned Q-Former aggregates backbone features into fixed-length tokens that condition the action expert.

03

Act in native coordinates

Dataset-specific state and action projections connect a shared transformer to each robot’s native interface.

STAGE 01

Video adaptation

Learn variable-view video representations from robot and human videos.

STAGE 02

Action pretraining

Freeze the video backbone and learn the Q-Former and action expert from action-labeled trajectories.

STAGE 03

Target-domain post-training

Jointly update the model on LIBERO or real-world demonstrations with video and action supervision.

03 / Evaluation

From benchmarks
to the real world.

Benchmark performance, distribution shifts, and real-robot trials reveal complementary aspects of the transferred representation.

81.7%

Mean real-world success

X-World succeeds in 98 of 120 trials across six tasks. Each policy uses the same 20 predefined initial placements per task; X-World achieves the highest reported success rate on every task.

98.5%

Mean LIBERO success

Evaluated on Spatial, Object, Goal, and Long with 50 episodes for each of 10 tasks per suite. The reported comparison includes methods with different pretraining data and compute budgets.

04 / Understanding the transfer

What makes the prior useful?

98.5% → 89.5%

Video adaptation matters

Removing Stage 1 lowers mean LIBERO success. The two pretraining stages interact: their effects are not simply additive.

98.5% → 83.1%

Cross-view fusion matters

Removing residual cross-view attention causes the largest drop among the reported component removals. On Fold Towel, success falls from 12/20 to 0/20.
